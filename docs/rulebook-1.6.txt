Single-Sleeve Storage
Pocket Paladin does not come with a box or a physical rulebook. This is in
part to save the customer money (boxes and rulebooks are expensive
components!). But it’s also because I had a unique vision for this game: I
wanted you to be able to carry it in your wallet. All essential components
for Pocket Paladin will fit into a single card sleeve, with the optional
reference cards in a second sleeve. A firm sleeve may not work for this. I
recommend using a soft (and typically cheaper) sleeve, such as Ultra Pro
penny sleeves. To store the game:

1.   Lay the cards on the table. Put a single layer of tokens and shards on
     top of 2 of the cards.
2.   Place the remaining 2 cards on top of those cards.
3.   Stack one of those piles on top of the other to make a single stack.
4.   Carefully slide the entire stack into a soft card sleeve.
5.   Slide the cover mat into the sleeve, its two short edges around the
     outside cards. This prevents the tokens and shards from falling out.
6.   Slide the reference cards into a separate card sleeve.

Gameplay Overview
Pocket Paladin is played in turns and rounds. A round consists of up to 6
turns, but may be fewer turns if your hero is wounded. You have a limited
number of rounds to reach and defeat the enemy castle.

Most of what you do in the game you accomplish using your Ability Card,
advancing your Ability marker and using up to 3 Abilities per turn. A
typical turn includes using Abilities, Skills and Allies to help you move into
a map hex and interact with a site it contains. Sites include monsters and
mages you must fight, storms you must navigate, and towns or caravans
you can interact with to do things like heal, recruit Allies and buy Crystals.

Your chosen difficulty setting determines your round limit. Each round is
either a day round or a night round. Each round is thematically half a day.

You win if you reach and defeat the enemy castle before time runs out!

Difficulty Settings
Before setting up your game, choose which difficulty setting you want. It
will affect your round limit and the type and number of enemies you face at
the castle. For your first game, we recommend playing on the easy
difficulty setting.

          o Time Limit: 6 rounds
          o Castle: 2 tower enemies
        Easy
          o Time Limit: 5 rounds
          o Castle: 2 tower enemies and 1 wandering enemy
        Normal
          o Time Limit: 4 rounds

        Hard

          o Castle: 3 tower enemies and 1 wandering enemy
        Expert

        Impossible
          o Time Limit: 3 rounds
          o Castle: 4 tower enemies

How to Win: Defeat the Castle
To win Pocket Paladin, you must reach the enemy castle and defeat its
defenders before time runs out. Thus, you will always want to be conscious
of moving Northward on your adventures. You need to level up your hero
throughout your journey to stand a chance against the powerful enemies
defending the castle. A winning strategy balances haste (so you don’t run
out of time) and interacting with map sites (so you can earn XP to level
up).

Important Concepts
A few concepts will be helpful to understand before learning the rest of
game.


Rounds, Turns, Actions, Abilities & Points
Each of these concepts will be explained in more detail later, but for now
it’s important to have an introduction to how they relate to each other.

Pocket Paladin is played over multiple Rounds, with a different Round
limit depending on your chosen difficulty setting. Each Round is made up
of multiple Turns (a maximum of 6, but sometimes less depending on how
many wounds you have). During each Turn, you will take Actions
(movement, combat, etc.). And finally, every Action you take will be
powered by Abilities (typically 1 to 3 Abilities per Action) or, sometimes,
Skills or Allies. Abilities are on the squares connected by arrows on your
Ability Card. Abilities generate Points, which dictate the effectiveness of
your Actions. You are only allowed to use a maximum of 3 Abilities per
Turn, but you can combine multiple Abilities of the same type to generate
more Points.

Note that Abilities are not the only thing that can power your Actions.
Allies and Skills may also contribute.


Crystals and the Source
Crystals are resources you can spend to use more powerful actions. There
are 3 crystal colors, shown below: Black (clubs icon, left), Blue (spades
icon, middle), and Purple (diamonds icon, right).




You track how many Crystals you have on the Crystal Tracking Shards,
shown below.

appropriate Shard to reflect the increase. When you spend a Crystal, rotate
the appropriate Shard to reflect the decrease. There is no limit to how long
you can keep Crystals. You may only ever have a maximum of 5 Crystals

                          The Crystal Source Shard (known as simply

                          free once per turn. During setup -- and after

Note that some orientations of the Source Shard have 2 Crystal types
shown, separated by a “/”. This means you have your choice of the 2 to use

Do not automatically reroll the Source Shard if you didn’t use a Crystal
from it on your turn. Don’t reroll the Source Shard until the Turn Cleanup

It is recommended that when the Source Crystal is available, you place the
Soure Shard on the left side of your Ability Card, as shown during setup.
After using the Source Crystal, move the Shard to the right side of the
Ability Card to remember this. When you reroll it for next turn, once again
place it on the left side of the Ability Card.


Choosing Random Tokens
Sometimes the rules will ask you to choose a random enemy token of a
given type (Monster/red or Mage/purple). To choose a random token, mix
up the appropriate token stack and choose one without looking.

Alternatively, I like to leave the tokens in their stacks and simply avoid
looking at the top tokens in detail. Then, I just grab the top token of the
appropriate stack when I need a random one. I find this easier than
shuffling up the tokens every time.

Rounds
You have a limited number of rounds (dictated by your difficulty setting) to
reach and defeat the castle. Each Round is thematically half a 24-hour day,
and every round is either a Day Round or a Night Round.

A Round consists of multiple Turns. You keep taking Turns until the Round
ends, after which you perform Round cleanup and start the next Round.

Rounds have 2 stages:

     1. Turns Stage, in which you take multiple turns.

     2. Round Cleanup Stage, which prepares you for the next round.


Turns Stage
The Turns Stage of the Round consists of you taking multiple Turns until
the Round ends and you perform cleanup during the Round Cleanup Stage.
After that, a new Round begins and you repeat the process.

Your Turn is made up of 2 phases. Complete both phases every turn, in this
order:

     1. Action Phase, in which you’ll use Abilities to perform Actions,
     as well as activate Skills and Allies.

     2. Turn Cleanup Phase, which prepares you for the next Turn.


ACTION PHASE
During the Action Phase, you can take any number of Actions that you
can or want to. You take most Actions by using Abilities to generate some
number of Points of one of the game’s Point types, and then spending
those Points on the Action.

First, we’ll explain how to generate Points. Then, we’ll go through the
different Actions you can take on your Turn, most of which require you to
spend some number of Points.


                                                                         11
GENERATING AND SPENDING POINTS
There are 4 Point types (some have multiple subtypes). The Point types and
subtypes are noted below.

        Movement
        Influence
        Attack (4 subtypes)
         o Physical Melee Attack
         o Physical Ranged Attack
         o Magic Melee Attack
         o Magic Ranged Attack
        Block (2 subtypes)
         o   Physical Block
         o   Magic Block

Think of Points as temporary currency that you generate so you can
immediately spend them on an Action. For example, you might generate 4
movement Points so you can move into an enemy’s hex. Then you might
generate 5 block Points to block the enemy’s attack, and then 4 attack
Points to defeat the enemy.

There is no physical means for tracking the Points you generate and spend.
You don’t need to remember them for long. You only generate Points to
complete the current Action, and any unspent Points are lost immediately
after the Action is completed.

How Long are Points Stored?
The Points you generate are stored until…
     You spend them.
     You decide to generate a different Point type, or take an Action
        that requires you to spend a different Point type.
     Your turn ends.

For example, let’s say you generate 6 movement Points. You spend 2 of
these movement Points to move into a grassland hex, which means you
have 4 remaining. Then you decide to interact with a town on that new hex,
so you use an Ability to generate 2 influence Points. This causes your 4
remaining movement Points to be lost.


12
If you had instead decided to skip interacting with the town and kept
moving, you could have spent your remaining 4 movement Points to move
into, say, an adjacent wasteland hex. That would have brought your
remaining movement Points down to zero. You would need to generate
more movement Points if you wanted to take another move Action.

NOTE: As shorthand, this rulebook and reference material may simply
refer to Points as the Point type. For example, instead of saying, “Spend 4
movement Points” this rulebook may simply say, “Spend 4 movement”.

Ways to Generate Points
There are 3 ways to generate Points.

    1.      Use an Ability (most common)
    2.      Use an Ally
    3.      Use a Skill

You can use a combination of the above methods for Point generation for a
single Action. For example, you could use an Ability to generate 2
movement and use an Ally to generate 4 movement, and then spend that 6
total movement on move Action.

Likewise, you can use multiple of the same method to generate Points for a
single Action. For example, you could use 2 consecutive Abilities to
generate 2 and 4 movement, for a total of 6 movement. Or, you could use 2
Allies to generate 2 and 3 attack, for a total of 5 attack.

Abilities
Abilities are the heart and soul of Pocket Paladin.
Abilities are found in the Ability squares on your
Ability Card. Most of what you do on your Turn
will be deciding how to best use Abilities during
your Turn to accomplish the Actions you want to
take.

Each Ability square has two Abilities on it: a top Ability and a bottom
Ability. You can use the top Ability for free, or the bottom Ability for a
cost. When you use an Ability, you get to use just one of the two options in
the Ability square. The top-half Ability is the square’s basic Ability, and
the bottom-half Ability is the square’s boosted Ability.



                                                                         13
Using Abilities
You can use up to 3 Abilities per Turn. You are allowed to use as few as
zero if desired.

You use an Ability by advancing your Ability marker from its existing
Ability square to a different Ability square connected by an arrow. The
arrow must point from the marker’s current square to the destination square.
The Ability square you moved the marker to is the one you get to use an
Ability from.

Each Ability square is connected to one (or more) other squares by colored
arrows. You can only move to Ability squares via arrows that match the
color of a hero Level you have already reached.

Each hero Level on has a colored border; either yellow, white or green. As
you increase your hero Level, you’ll move the Level marker rightward. As
soon as you reach a white-bordered Level, you have unlocked access to the
white arrows on the Ability Card. When you reach a green-bordered Level,
you have unlocked access to green arrows on the Ability Card.

As you can see from the image on the next page at the start of the game
you only have access to Ability squares connected by yellow arrows (A).
When you reach the third hero Level, you unlock the Ability squares
connected by white arrows (B) in addition to the yellow ones you already
had access to. When you reach the fifth hero Level, you unlock access to
Ability Squares connected by green arrows (C) and can now use all Ability
squares on the card.

Note that this example is showing the red Paladin’s Tracks and Ability
Cards, but the same principle applies to the purple Paladin’s cards. Just pay
attention to which arrow colors you have unlocked to see which Ability
squares you have access to.

Combining Abilities
As you are generating Points to carry out an Action, you are allowed to
combine consecutive Abilities you use towards the same Action. An
Abilities are considered consecutive if they are connected directly by an
arrow (with no abilities in between).

For example, if you use an Ability that generates 2 movement Points and
from there immediately use a different Ability that generates 4 movement
Points, you can use that total of 6 movement Points toward the same
Action.

You are not allowed to combine non-consecutive Abilities toward the
same Action. If in the above example you had used an Ability that
generated influence Points in between the two movement-generating
Abilities, you could not use those two movement-generating Abilities
toward the same Action.

Note that you will learn there are different attack and block types (physical
and magic), as well as different attack ranges (ranged and melee). You are
allowed to combine consecutive attack abilities of any type. Likewise, you
are allowed to combine consecutive block abilities of any type.

For example, you could use an Ability that generates 2 physical block
Points, and next use an Ability that generates 4 magic block Points. These
could be considered consecutive abilities and combined for blocking a
single enemy, even though they are different types of block.

Allies
Allies are companions you can recruit to
help you on your adventure. Allies exist on
the opposite face of the Enemy Tokens, and
on the top half of that face (the various
Skills are on the bottom half). Allies have
their image on the very top. Below their
image is the Ally’s 3 stats. From left to right:
Recruitment Cost (A), Effect (B), HP (C).

The Recruitment Cost (A) is how much Influence you must spend to
recruit the ally. You can recruit allies at specific Sites, namely Towns and
Caravans. When you recruit an Ally, choose from among the Allies
currently in the Offer (refill the Offer back up to 3 Allies after your turn in
the same way you populated the Offer during setup). After recruiting an
Ally, put it above your Tracks Card. You can optionally tuck the bottom
part of the Ally token underneath your Tracks Card to cover everything
except the Ally’s information. The Ally in the example image has a
Recruitment Cost of 8 Influence.

You can recruit as many Allies as you can afford, but you can only keep as
many Allies as you have capacity for. Your Ally capacity is the sum of all
Ally capacity icons (     ) present on your current Level and all Levels
below it. You can choose to replace existing Allies with new Allies you
recruit, even if the Ally you replace is exhausted or wounded (newly
recruited Allies always join you ready (unexhausted) and unwounded).
Replaced Allies are placed at the bottom of the appropriate Enemy stack.

The Ally’s Effect (B) is what you can exhaust them to generate. At any
time on your turn, you can exhaust an ally by moving them to the right side
of your Tracks Card. (Note that exhausted Skills will also go to this area, so
keep exhausted allies near the top half of the card and exhausted skills near
the bottom half so you don’t confuse them). Exhausting an Ally in this way
generates Points as shown by their Effect. Ally Effects are colored red or
purple to help show whether it generates Physical (red) or Magic (purple)
Points, but you can also see this simply from the Effect’s icon. The Ally in
the example image has an Effect of generating 7 Physical Melee Attack.
                                                                            17
You cannot use an Exhausted Ally for its Effect again this round.
Exhausted Allies are readied (unexhausted) during Round Cleanup, so you
get to use them again next round. Even though you can’t use them for their
Effects, exhausted Allies can still be wounded.

The Ally’s HP (C) is how much Enemy damage they can soak when
wounded (see Wounding Allies on page 28). The Ally in the example
image has an HP of 6.

Skills
Skills are special abilities you earn when you
reach certain hero Levels. Skills are found in
the bottom section of the Ally/Skill tokens, on
the opposite face of all Enemy tokens.

When you increase your Level and see the skill
capacity icon (       ), you immediately gain a
Skill. (Note that the red Paladin begins the game with a Skill because her
starting Level already has a skill capacity icon).

When you gain a Skill, choose a skill token from the Offer and place it
below your Tracks Card. You may optionally tuck the top part of the token
underneath your Tracks Card to cover the Ally information.

Each Skill has a different effect, represented with icons. You can see
descriptions of each Skill’s effect on page 39, as well as on the reference
cards provided with the game.

Some Skills have passive effects that give you a benefit that is always in
play.

Some Skills require you to exhaust them to be used (shown with          ). To
use a Skill with an exhaust cost, the Skill must be ready, which means it is
below your Tracks Card. You exhaust a Skill by moving it to the right side
of your Tracks Card, keeping it closer to the bottom half of the card so you
don’t confuse the token with exhausted Allies. Exhausted Skills cannot be
used again, but you ready all exhausted Skills during Round Cleanup, so
you’ll get to use them again next round.

Some Skills require you to spend an Ability to use (shown with             ).
Using one of these Skills requires you to give up one of the 3 Abilities for
your turn. In other words, if you use one of these Skills on your turn, you

TAKING ACTIONS
On your Turn, you can take as many Actions as you can or want to. There
are many different Actions you can take, but most of the time you will be
either moving or interacting with a map site.

You take most Actions by generating and spending Points. Remember that
you can generate Points with a combination of using Abilities, Skills and
Allies (and remember you can use a maximum of 3 Abilities per turn!).

Below are the different Actions you can take and how to resolve them.


Move
Move Actions are how your hero gets from one place to another. To take a
move Action, carry out the following steps:

    1.   Generate movement Points.

    2.   Spend those movement Points to move your hero token into an
         adjacent hex, reducing your stored movement Points by the
         movement cost of the hex you entered. The cost to move into a
         hex is based on terrain type. Terrain types are explained in the
         next section.

    3.   Continue moving until you choose to stop or cannot move any
         further, or until you move into a hex with a required site.
         Optional sites do not force you to stop movement or interact with
         the site. The different sites and whether they are optional or
         required are listed beginning on page 21.

Exploring
When you have reached one of the Northern-most full hexes on the
Northern-most map card, your hero will be adjacent to one of the half
                                                                           19
hexes along the card’s Northern border. Before moving any further, you
must Explore.

To Explore, you must spend 2 Movement while your hero token is in one
of the last full hexes along the Northern border of the second map card.
After spending this Movement, flip over the first map card (bottom-most
map card) and place it North of the map card your hero is currently on. The
card you flipped is now showing one of the two Castles. Tuck this card
underneath the previous map card so the partial hexes on the previous map
card line up and connect with the card you just placed.
If there are any tokens on the map card you need to flip and reposition,
discard these tokens to the bottom of their respective Enemy stacks.

After placing the Castle map card, populate the card with any Wandering
Monster or Mage tokens as needed.

If after spending the 2 Movement to Explore you have any Movement
Points still stored, you may now spend these to continue moving if desired.

Discovering Tower Enemies
Unlike Wandering Enemies, Tower Enemy tokens are not placed on the
map automatically during setup or when a new map card with Tower
Enemy sites is revealed. Instead, you reveal them as you get close to them--
but depending on whether it is a Day or Night.

If it is a Day Round, place the appropriate type of enemy token as soon as
you move into a hex adjacent to a hex with a Monster or Mage Tower.

If it is a Night Round, you only reveal Tower Enemies after you move in to
the Tower’s hex. You therefore must immediately enter combat with the
token after revealing it.

Wandering and Tower Enemies use the same enemy tokens, but you make
some modifications to their stats and XP rewards depending on which type
the token is.

Enemy tokens on Wandering Monster or Mage sites are always Wandering

Combat Phases
Combat has 4 phases. Resolve them in order:

1. Ranged Attack Phase
2. Block Phase
3. Melee Attack Phase
4. Resolution Phase

1. Ranged Phase

In the Ranged Phase, you can use Ranged Attacks (     and     generated
from Abilities, Skills, and/or Allies), which gives you the potential to
defeat the enemy before they have a chance to attack.

If the enemy has Physical Resistance (         ), you must halve the Physical
attack Points (     ) you use against it. If the enemy has Magic Resistance
(     ), you must halve the Magic attack Points (      ) you use against it.

If you can generate enough ranged attack equal to or greater than the
enemy’s HP, you defeat it. Proceed directly to the Resolution Phase.

If you did not defeat the enemy in the Ranged Phase, proceed to the Block
Phase.
26
2. Block Phase
The enemy will now attack you using its attack stat. You must either block
the attack or take wounds.

If you spend Block Points equal to or greater than the enemy’s attack value,
you have successfully blocked the attack. Proceed to the Melee Attack
Phase. You are not required to spend any Block Points in this phase; you
can choose to simply let the attack through and take wounds if you want to.

Important: Blocking is all or nothing. You must block the full amount of
the enemy’s attack for the block to be successful. If you are short even 1
Point, the block is unsuccessful and the enemy’s full attack gets through.

If the block was unsuccessful, you must take wounds.

TAKING WOUNDS
To take wounds, first determine how many you must
take by comparing your Armor stat with the enemy’s
Attack stat. Your current Armor stat is found by looking
at your current hero Level, on the top-right corner of the
Level box. In the image on the right: if this was your
current hero Level, you can see you have an Armor stat
of 3.

Divide the enemy’s Attack stat by your Armor value to find the number of
wounds you receive. If there is any remainder, round up to the next whole
number.

For example, if the enemy’s Attack is 7 and your Armor is 3, you’d
calculate it as: 7 divided by 3 equals 2.33. You round up to the nearest
whole number to land at 3. Therefore, you must take 3 wounds.

For every wound you must take, shift the wound
marker on your Wound Track leftward 1 space.
The example on the right shows what it would
look like to take 3 wounds if you had been at full
health, with the wound marker starting in the
rightmost space on the Wound Track.

If you take a Wound that pushes the Wound marker onto or past the Turn
space your Turn Marker is on, it means this is your last Turn of the Round
(see page 31).

                                                                         27
If you take enough Wounds to push the Wound marker past the Turn space
your Turn marker is on, you must take additional Wounds! For every Turn
space your Wound marker has moved beyond where your Turn marker
currently is, you must take 1 additional Wound (after taking all your
normal Wounds). This rule is typically most relevant during Castle combat
(see page 33), in which you are facing multiple enemies and have the
potential to take a large number of wounds.

If your Wound marker ever moves into the leftmost space of the Wound
track, you lose the game immediately.

Wounding Allies: You can wound ready or exhausted Allies to soak up
some of the enemy damage for you. If you fail to block an enemy’s attack,
you may move one or more Allies to the left side of your Tracks Card (the
Wounded area) to wound them. Then, reduce the enemy’s attack by the
HP(s) of the Ally(s) you wounded. Your hero then takes wounds based on
whatever attack was not soaked up by wounding the Ally(s).

HEALING WOUNDS
Some effects in the game provide Healing, such as ending your turn on a
Healing Garden or using certain Skills. Healing is how you get rid of
Wounds. For every Point of Healing you spend, move the Wound marker
rightward one space.

You may heal an ally by spending Healing Points equal to half their HP stat,
rounded down.

EFFICIENT AND INEFFICIENT BLOCKS
All enemy attacks and blocks have a type: Physical or Magic. Monsters
(    ) deal Physical ( ) attack, and Mages (  ) deal Magic (  ) attack.

If a block type matches the attack type, then the block is considered to be
Efficient. This means the block gets to block at a 1:1 ratio; that is, 1 Point
of block gets to block 1 Point of enemy attack. Physical block (          ) is
Efficient against Physical attack (   ), and Magic block (      ) is Efficient
against Magic attack (    ).

If the block type doesn’t match the enemy attack type, then it is Inefficient.
It requires 2 Points of Inefficient block to block 1 Point of enemy attack.

It’s possible you will spend different types of block (some Physical and
some Magic) to block an enemy’s attack. This is allowed, but you still must
abide the rules about Efficient and Inefficient blocks. Deal with the two
types of block separately in terms of halving them for Inefficiency or not.
28
For example, let’s say you are facing a Mage enemy with 4 attack. Since it
is a Mage enemy, it deals Magic attack. You use an Ability to generate 2
Magic block, and then use another Ability to generate 4 Physical block.
This is just enough for a successful Block. The 4 Physical block is
Inefficient against the enemy’s Magic attack, so it requires 4 Points to
block just 2 attack. The Inefficient Physical block (for a modified 2 block)
and the Magic block (for 2 Efficient block) add up to the 4 block you
needed for the attack.

Whether you successfully blocked the enemy’s attack or not, you now
move on to the Melee Phase. You must spend attack Points equal to or
greater than the enemy’s HP to defeat it.

In the Melee Phase, you are allowed to use Ranged attacks. You can even
use a combination of Ranged attack Points and Melee attack Points.

If the enemy has Physical Resistance (   ), you must halve the Physical
attack Points (     ,     ) you use against it. If the enemy has Magic
Resistance (    ), you must halve the Magic attack Points ( ,    ) you
use against it.

If you defeated the enemy, place its token to the right of your Abilities Card.

token upside-down when saving it as XP to denote that you get the larger,
upside-down XP reward. If you now have enough XP to level up, you may

If you could not spend enough attack to defeat the enemy, you have lost the
combat. You must retreat, which means moving your hero token to the hex
from which you moved into the enemy’s hex. The enemy token remains in

     round. If you meet either condition, proceed to the Round Cleanup

      a) Your Turn marker was on space 6 of the Turn Track this turn

          advance to).

     b) The space you moved your Turn marker into on the Turn Track is
        blocked by the Wound marker. The Wound marker blocks a Turn

        into. In the image above, you can see the hero has taken 5 wounds

Castle Combat (Multiple Enemies)
When you move into a hex adjacent to the Castle, you reveal all enemy
tokens at the Castle. The number of enemies you face -- and whether they
are Wandering or Tower enemies or some combination of the two -- is
determined by your difficulty setting.

While in combat with multiple enemies, complete each combat stage
(Ranged, Block, Melee) for all enemies before moving on to the next phase.

By default, treat each enemy individually. You are not allowed to block or
attack multiple enemies at once.

Another way to explain it: For each phase of combat, you must generate
Points to deal with 1 enemy, spend those points, and then move on to the
next enemy. Points generated for one enemy cannot be applied to a
different enemy, even if you have surplus Points remaining after dealing
with a given enemy.

                                                                          33
The exception to this rule is you are allowed to group enemies when
attacking them, whether in the Ranged Phase or Melee Phase. Grouping
enemies for the Block Phase is not allowed; you must deal with each
enemy’s attack separately.

You can choose which and how many enemies to group. Not all enemies in
the combat must be included in the group.

When you group enemies, combine their HPs and deal with just the new
sum total HP. Generate and spend attack Points for these multiple enemies
as if this was now a single enemy’s HP you were dealing with.

For example, if you group two enemies, one with 4 HP and the other with 5
HP, it is now as if you are facing a single enemy’s HP of 9 HP. You must
defeat this 9 HP in the normal all-or-nothing fashion to defeat the two
grouped enemies.

If any enemies in the group have Physical Resistance, all your Physical
attacks against this group are halved.

If any enemies in the group have Magic Resistance, all your Magic attacks
against this group are halved.

If any enemies in the group have the Fortified ability, you cannot attack
this group during the Ranged Phase (remember, the Castle is already a
Fortified site. An enemy at a Fortified site that also has the Fortified ability
cannot be attacked during the Ranged Phase, not even using Precision).

You will likely end up taking Wounds from some enemies and blocking
others. You may also end up defeating some enemies and not defeating
others. Save defeated enemy tokens for their XP reward as normal. You
cannot level up during a combat, but you may level up after the combat if
you have enough XP.

If you defeat all enemies at the Castle, you win the game immediately!

If you did not defeat all enemies at the Castle, you must end your turn and
retreat to the hex from which you approached the Castle. If are not out of
time, you can move back into the Castle hex next turn to take on the
enemies you have not yet defeated.
